# Launch review: Bajra Bowl

**Do not launch yet**

The product solves a real problem for caregivers of diabetics, but the current pricing model is rejected by 93% of respondents. You must pivot from a daily breakfast item to a premium medical nutrition product for elders.

## Where it fails

### Price point is rejected by the target demographic (high severity)

The proposed single-cup price (₹79) is roughly 3x to 5x the cost of home-cooked alternatives (₹15-₹25). Respondents explicitly state this gap is 'not feasible' or 'not logical' for their budgets.

*Evidence:* 11 out of 15 respondents selected 'only_on_offer' in q_price_threshold. 9 out of 15 selected 'cannot_justify_the_gap' in q_homemade_substitution. 14 out of 15 selected 'false' for would_buy_at_launch.

### Misalignment between buyer and user (high severity)

The product is positioned as a breakfast for working adults, but the primary purchase intent is for elderly family members. Marketing it as a general breakfast ignores the specific motivation of the buyer.

*Evidence:* 10 out of 15 respondents selected 'for_elder_family_member' in q_purchase_target, compared to only 4 for 'for_myself'.

### Distrust of packaged health claims (medium severity)

A significant portion of the audience is skeptical of 'diabetic-friendly' labels on processed food, which creates a barrier to entry despite the need.

*Evidence:* 3 out of 15 respondents selected 'claim_distrust' in q_health_claim_framing. One respondent stated: 'I am very skeptical of health claims on packaged foods, especially those claiming to be diabetic-friendly.'

## What is working

- **Strong pull from the diabetic-friendly positioning** 10 out of 15 respondents selected 'claim_draws_me_in' in q_health_claim_framing. One respondent noted: 'I manage a chronic condition myself, so health claims regarding blood sugar are very important to me.'
- **Clear channel preference for quick commerce** 10 out of 15 respondents selected 'quick_commerce' in q_channel_preference.

## Where it could still win

### Adults caring for elderly parents or spouses with diabetes

This group (67% of respondents) is looking for convenient, safe options for their elders and is less sensitive to price than the general working adult, provided the health benefit is trusted.

*Do this:* Reposition the product as 'Care Nutrition' rather than 'Instant Breakfast'. Focus marketing on the peace of mind for the caregiver.

### High-income urban professionals managing blood sugar

A small subset (4 respondents) indicated they would buy for themselves. They value the time savings on 'rushed mornings' but need the price to feel like a premium health investment, not an expensive snack.

*Do this:* Target this segment exclusively via D2C with subscription models that lower the effective unit cost.

## Before you launch

1. **Reposition the brand from 'Instant Breakfast' to 'Diabetic Care Nutrition'.** The data shows 10 buyers want it for elders, not themselves. The current 'breakfast' pitch fails to convert the 9 people who 'cannot justify the gap' for a regular meal.
2. **Abandon the ₹79 single-cup price point for retail; launch exclusively with the ₹199 trial pack and subscription.** 11 respondents will only buy 'on offer'. The ₹199 trial pack (₹50/unit) aligns with this sensitivity. Use the trial pack to acquire the caregiver segment.
3. **Replace the 'diabetic-friendly' text claim with third-party certification or doctor endorsements on the front of the pack.** 3 respondents expressed 'distrust' of the claim. Respondents cited 'doctor or family recommendation' (7 votes) as a top trigger, higher than the trial pack (6 votes). You need external validation to overcome skepticism.
4. **Pause general trade plans and focus distribution on Quick Commerce.** 10 respondents prefer quick_commerce vs 4 for local_shop. Go where the urgency for the caregiver is highest.

## What this survey cannot tell you

- We do not know if the product actually tastes good or if the 3-minute texture is acceptable, as no one has tasted it.
- The sample is skewed towards cost-sensitive and rural demographics (e.g., persona 0148, 0192), which may explain the extreme price sensitivity, but we lack data on the wealthy urban demographic who might pay ₹79 easily.
- We do not know the specific 'trigger events' that lead to a purchase (e.g., a diagnosis, a doctor's visit) beyond general recommendations.
