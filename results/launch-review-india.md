# Launch review: Bajra Bowl

**Do not launch yet**

The product has a strong health hook but a fatal pricing gap; 85% of respondents cannot justify the cost over home-cooked alternatives.

Written by `gemma4:31b-cloud` from 7 survey responses across `bajra-gemma4cloud-n8`. The numbers are pulled out of the job output and you can check them. The judgement is the model's, and it is worth as much as you think a sample this size and a model like this are worth.

## Where it fails

### Price-to-value proposition is rejected (high severity)

The ₹79 price point is perceived as too high compared to the ₹15-25 home-cooked cost.

*Evidence:* 6 out of 7 respondents chose 'cannot_justify_the_gap'; 3 respondents stated they would 'walk away' because it is too expensive.

### Low purchase intent at launch (high severity)

Despite interest in the concept, the actual willingness to buy is nearly non-existent.

*Evidence:* 6 out of 7 respondents answered 'false' to 'would_buy_at_launch'.

## What is working

- **Strong appeal of the health claim** 5 out of 7 respondents stated the 'diabetic-friendly' claim draws them in.
- **High interest in low-friction trial** 5 out of 7 respondents identified the ₹199 trial pack as the primary trigger for purchase.

## Where it could still win

### Caregivers for elderly family members

More respondents are buying for others than for themselves, specifically citing elder care.

*Do this:* Pivot marketing from 'busy professional' to 'healthy care for parents' and test a 'care-package' bundle.

## Before you launch

1. **Drastically reduce the single-cup price or shift to a value-pack only model.** 6 out of 7 respondents explicitly stated the 3x cost over home-cooked food is not feasible.
2. **Lead with the ₹199 trial pack as the primary acquisition tool on Quick-Commerce.** Quick-commerce is the preferred channel (4/7) and the trial pack is the strongest purchase trigger (5/7).

## What this survey cannot tell you

- Taste preference (respondents only saw a brief, did not sample the product).
- Willingness to pay for specific variants (Masala Khichdi vs. Ragi Porridge).
- Actual conversion rate of 'interest' to 'purchase' in a live environment.
